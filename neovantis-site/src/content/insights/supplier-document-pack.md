A request for "the documents" can mean very different things depending on who is asking. Procurement may want a licence and a quality certificate. The CSSD may want reprocessing instructions. Clinical engineering may want a service manual. Sending one generic pack to all of them usually leads to a second round of emails.

This article separates the two broad kinds of document, explains what each one does and does not show, and suggests how to ask for the right ones first time. It reflects South African requirements as stated in the primary sources linked below.

## Two kinds of document

| | Supplier-level documents | Product-level documents |
|---|---|---|
| **What they describe** | The company and its authorised activities or systems | A specific product |
| **Examples** | SAHPRA medical device establishment licence; ISO 13485 certificate; B-BBEE certificate | Product information or specification sheet; instructions for use; labelling; material statements (e.g. latex); user-care or reprocessing instructions; manuals |
| **Who often asks** | Procurement, supplier onboarding, tender evaluation | Theatre, CSSD, clinical engineering, infection control |

Both matter, and they answer different questions. A supplier-level document cannot stand in for product information, and the reverse is also true.

## Supplier-level documents

### SAHPRA establishment licence

SAHPRA issues three types of medical device establishment licence: manufacturer, distributor and wholesaler. The type depends on the activities performed. For example, packaging, labelling, servicing or refurbishing devices count as manufacturing activities, and a wholesaler's licence does not permit import or export ([SAHPRA guideline SAHPGL-MD-07, May 2025][md07]). Applicants list the medical devices they manufacture, distribute or wholesale as part of the application ([SAHPRA medical devices page][md-page]).

What the licence does **not** show is that each product has been individually registered. SAHPRA states that the registration process for medical devices "is still in development" ([medical devices page][md-page]). In a 2025 communication, it said it "has not yet called up medical devices … for product registration", and that companies apply for an establishment licence that includes a listing of their devices ([SAHPRA MD08, 2025][md08]). National Treasury's medical equipment contract anticipates this changing. Once devices are called up for registration in the Government Gazette, bidders will need to provide product registration certificates as well ([RT2-2025, clause 6.3.3.1][rt2]).

You can check whether a company holds a licence on SAHPRA's "Medical Devices Licences Issued" database, by licence number or company name ([SAHPGL-MD-07][md07]).

### ISO 13485 certificate

ISO 13485 is the standard for medical device quality management systems. SAHPRA's guidance says ISO 13485 will become compulsory for manufacturers and distributors upon communication from SAHPRA ([SAHPGL-MD-07][md07]).

When reviewing a certificate, check **whose** it is and **what it covers**. Tender documents often ask for the manufacturer's certificate specifically. The national surgical instruments contract requires that "the holder of the certificates must be the original product/equipment manufacturer" ([RT252-2025, clause 6.6.3.1][rt252]). The Western Cape consumables bid asks for a valid ISO 13485 certificate "for each manufacturer whose products form part of their bid" ([WCGHCC0001/2025, section 2.7][wc1]).

A quality certificate shows that an organisation's system was assessed against the standard, for the scope stated on the certificate. It is not approval of every product that organisation supplies.

### Other supplier documents

Depending on your process, procurement may also ask for B-BBEE status and, for public-sector work, registration on the Central Supplier Database: the Western Cape consumables bid requires all bidders to be registered on the CSD at bid closing ([WCGHCC0001/2025, section 2.4][wc1]). Where the supplier is not the manufacturer, a letter from the manufacturer may also be needed. The same bid asks for written proof that the manufacturer has no objection to the bid and will continue to supply for the contract period ([WCGHCC0001/2025, section 2.6][wc1]).

## Product-level documents

Product documents answer the practical questions of the people who use, reprocess and maintain the product. Examples from South African tender documents include:

- **Product information in English.** The national medical equipment contract asks for catalogues, operating manuals and instruction leaflets in at least English ([RT2-2025, clause 5.2.5.3][rt2]).
- **User-care and reprocessing information** for reusable items, such as cleaning, disinfection or sterilisation methods ([RT2-2025, clause 5.2.3.6][rt2]). For loan instrument sets, the CFSA sterile services procedures expect cleaning and sterilising instructions and tray checklists to accompany the set ([CFSA SOPs, 2024][cfsa]).
- **Labelling** that shows the manufacturer, item code, batch or lot number, expiry and, for sterile items, sterilisation details ([WCGHCC0001/2025, section 3.6][wc1]).
- **Material statements**, such as latex-free confirmation where it is specified ([WCGHCC0001/2025, section 2.11][wc1]).
- **Service manuals and maintenance procedures** for equipment. The Western Cape equipment bid treats service manuals as mandatory, along with detailed preventive maintenance and calibration procedures ([WCGHCC0047/2025][wc47]).

## Who asks for what

| Team | Typical documents | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Procurement and supplier onboarding | Establishment licence, ISO 13485 certificate, B-BBEE, manufacturer letter | To approve the supplier and meet policy |
| Theatre and clinicians | Product information, instructions for use, compatibility information | To confirm the product suits the intended use |
| CSSD | Reprocessing instructions, tray lists, photographs | To process and check instrument sets safely |
| Clinical and biomedical engineering | Operating and service manuals, maintenance and calibration procedures, training information | To commission and maintain equipment |
| Infection prevention and control | Material statements, cleaning and disinfection information | To assess risks and cleaning compatibility |

These are typical patterns, not fixed rules. Your facility's own process decides who needs what.

## How to ask once

A single, clear request saves time on both sides. Include:

1. **The products**, listed by name and specification
2. **The documents**, split into supplier-level and product-level
3. **The purpose**, for example supplier onboarding, product evaluation, a tender submission or equipment commissioning
4. **The format**, for example a certified copy, a PDF or a link
5. **The deadline**
6. **The person** who will review them

If a document does not exist for a product, or a requirement does not apply, ask the supplier to say so in writing. That is more useful than silence.
