"Is it available?" sounds like a single question. For a planned case it is usually three: are the implants available, are the instruments needed to place them available, and can both be at the hospital, processed and ready, on the right day?

This article follows one planned case from the first enquiry to confirmed requirements. It is about supply planning, not surgical technique. Clinical decisions, including the choice of implant, remain with the treating surgeon.

## Why the implant is only part of the answer

Many implant systems are designed to be used with their own instruments, held in trays as a set. Where those instruments are supplied as a loan set rather than kept permanently at the hospital, the same set may also be needed for cases elsewhere.

Even when a set is free, it is not ready the moment it arrives. In South Africa, the [CSSD Forums of South Africa (CFSA) Standard Operating Procedures][cfsa] expect loan sets to pass through a full validated decontamination process at the receiving hospital before they go to theatre. They set out the steps around that:

- the CSSD is notified of the surgery date, surgeon, procedure and loan equipment at least a day before delivery
- the set reaches the decontamination area at a very minimum four hours before use, with more time for larger sets
- the set is checked for completeness against its tray list, and missing or damaged instruments are reported to the supplier

So an implant can be in stock while the matching set is in use elsewhere, in transit or being reprocessed. That is why it helps to ask about both, for the same date.

## A worked example

> **Illustrative example.** The facility, team and case below are invented to show the planning steps. They do not describe a real hospital or patient, or any specific Neovantis arrangement.

A spinal surgery team at a regional hospital has an elective case booked in three weeks. The surgeon plans to use a particular screw and rod system the team has used before.

### Step 1: The first enquiry

The theatre coordinator sends the supplier a short enquiry:

- **System:** the system name as it appears on the hospital's records or the last tray list
- **Components:** screw and rod components, with any sizes or ranges the surgeon has already indicated
- **Instruments:** the instrument set for that system, and whether the hospital holds any of it already
- **Date:** the planned theatre date, and how firm it is
- **Contact:** the coordinator's name and number for questions

The enquiry contains no patient names or scans. A supplier can check availability without them.

### Step 2: Checking implants and instruments separately

The supplier checks two things: whether the components are available, and whether an instrument set for the system is free for that date with enough time to reach the hospital. If the set is committed elsewhere on that day, the team learns about it while there is still time to adjust.

### Step 3: Agreeing the timing

The coordinator and supplier agree when the set will be delivered, keeping in mind the hospital's own CSSD cut-off and the CFSA minimums. The coordinator lets the CSSD know what is coming, as the CFSA SOPs expect.

### Step 4: Handling a change

A week before the case, the date moves by two days. The coordinator tells the supplier straight away and asks for confirmation that both implants and set can move. A change to the date is effectively a new availability check.

### Step 5: Before and after the case

When the set arrives, the CSSD checks it against the tray list. The CFSA SOPs list what a loan set company is expected to provide with the set, including the number of trays, details of the prostheses supplied, cleaning and sterilising instructions, and tray checklists with photographs. After the case, the set is cleaned, checked and returned with the documentation the SOPs describe.

## How this looks in other specialities

The same principle, that the implant and its instruments need to line up, applies beyond spinal surgery. The details to share differ.

| Speciality | What to share |
|---|---|
| Spinal surgery | The system name; screw and rod or cervical components; the instrument set; the planned date |
| Cranial fixation | Whether you need plates, screws, titanium mesh or a combination; the instruments or tray that go with them; the planned date |
| Orthopaedics (K-wires) | Wire diameter and length; quantity; packaging or sterility requirements; whether the wires are for a specific case or for stock |

For K-wires, the question is often less about a loan set and more about whether the right diameters and lengths are on the shelf for the list. It is still worth saying when you need them.

## What to share when planning a case

1. **The system or product** you intend to use, as named on your records or tray lists
2. **The components** and any sizes or ranges already known
3. **The instruments or tray** you need with them, and what your hospital already holds
4. **The date**, how firm it is, and any CSSD cut-off time
5. **A named contact** who can answer questions and receive updates
6. **No patient information.** Supply planning does not need names, scans or records.

And three questions worth asking back:

- Are the implants **and** the matching instruments available for this date?
- When will the set arrive, and will it come with a tray list and reprocessing instructions?
- If the date changes, who confirms the new arrangements?
