Radiofrequency (RF) ablation equipment and its consumables work together. Electrodes, cannulae and other accessories have to suit the generator they are used with, so a supply enquiry is easier to answer when it starts with the platform.

This guide is for interventional pain management teams, theatre staff and procurement departments preparing an enquiry about RF equipment or consumables. It does not cover clinical technique, treatment settings or patient selection.

## Start with your platform

The most useful detail you can give is the make and model of the RF generator you use, or the one you are considering. With that information, a supplier can check which electrodes, cannulae and accessories are compatible.

If your facility uses more than one generator, list each one and say which the enquiry relates to.

## Say whether you are evaluating or ordering

Equipment and consumables enquiries tend to need different information.

**If you are evaluating a generator**, it helps to explain:

- the platform you use now, if any
- what your team needs the equipment to do, in operational terms
- whether you need product information or technical documentation for an internal evaluation
- any training or technical support questions you want answered before a decision

**If you are ordering consumables**, it helps to include:

- the generator platform the consumables are for
- the electrodes or cannulae you need, with any specifications you already know
- quantities, and whether this is a one-off order or regular replenishment
- the date you are working towards

## Raise training and support questions directly

If training, technical support or equipment arrangements matter to your decision, include them as specific questions in your enquiry. Arrangements depend on the equipment and the supplier, so it is better to ask than to assume.

## Ask for the documentation you need

Biomedical engineering and procurement teams may need product information or technical documentation before equipment is accepted or consumables are added to stock. State what your process requires so the supplier can tell you what can be provided.

## What not to include

A supply enquiry does not need patient information. Please do not include patient names, images, scans or clinical records. Questions about treatment settings or clinical technique should be directed to the appropriate clinical and manufacturer resources, not to a supply enquiry.

## A short checklist

Before you send an RF enquiry, check that it includes:

1. Generator make and model, current or under consideration
2. Whether you are evaluating equipment, ordering consumables or both
3. The electrodes or cannulae you need, with known specifications
4. Quantities and the date you are working towards
5. Any documentation, training or technical support questions
6. Your facility and the best contact person
